# Long form

Length is earned per minute. The failure mode is not being boring at the start,
it is a middle that stops advancing.

## Structure in sections

Plan three to six sections, each with its own small arc, and mark them on the
timeline with `add_marker` so the edit, the review and the user all see the same
skeleton. Each section must change what the viewer knows; a section that only
elaborates the previous one should be merged into it.

Signpost transitions with a consequence, not a menu: "which is why the first
approach failed" rather than "now let's look at approach two".

## Rhythm

- Vary density. A dense explanatory passage needs a lighter one after it — an
  example, a demonstration, a beat of silence.
- Vary the visual mode between sections: talking head, screen recording, b-roll,
  a diagram. Twelve minutes in one mode is what makes long videos feel long.
- Keep a recurring anchor so variation does not become drift: the same type, the
  same music family, the same framing for the speaker.
- Give the important claims room. Rushing the payoff to save thirty seconds
  wastes the ten minutes that led to it.

## Cut length, do not pad it

If the material runs short, cut to the shorter length. Stretched holds, slow
pushes on stills, recapping what was just said, and a long outro are all
padding, and viewers read padding as a lack of confidence.

If it runs long, cut whole sections rather than trimming everywhere. A tight
eight minutes beats a thinned-out twelve.

## Chapters and navigation

Long videos get skimmed. Markers you set become chapters; name them for what the
section delivers, not "Part 3". Make each section's opening frame recognisable
enough to be a landing point.

## Working method

Build the spine first — narration or interview audio — then picture, then
titles, then the mix. Checkpoint between stages (see checkpoint-discipline) and
review section by section with `render_contact_sheet` rather than only at the end:
a structural problem found at minute ten is cheap; found at delivery it is a
re-edit.
