# Taste direction

Generic output is not a rendering problem, it is a decision problem: nobody
decided what this video is, so every step fell back on its default. Decide
first, in one sentence.

## Write a read, not adjectives

A read names the subject, the audience and the feeling, and it rules things
out:

- Good: "Investor-facing launch: precise and restrained, credibility over
  excitement — no hype visuals, no stock-smiling."
- Good: "Security incident explainer: tense and surgical, high contrast,
  evidence must stay readable."
- Useless: "modern and clean", "cinematic", "professional", "engaging".

**The swap test:** if replacing the title would make this read fit any other
video, it is not a read yet. Rewrite it until it only fits this one.

## Set three dials, 1-10, before you build

- **Variation** — how much the visual grammar may change between beats. Low
  means one repeated frame language; high means each section may look distinct
  and needs stronger anchors (recurring type, colour, sound).
- **Motion** — calm holds and small transitions at the low end, constant
  kinetic movement at the high end.
- **Density** — one idea per frame at the low end, layered callouts and
  dashboards at the high end.

The dials have to be consistent: high motion with high density is unreadable —
pick which one carries the meaning. Low motion with high density needs long
holds so the frame can be read.

## Then hold the line

Say the read and the dials in the chat before building. Afterwards they decide
arguments: a transition, an overlay or a music cue that does not serve the read
gets dropped, even when it looks good on its own.

## Refuse the defaults

Flag and change these when you catch yourself doing them:

- Purple-to-blue gradient backgrounds chosen because nothing else was chosen.
- The same transition on every cut when variation is 4 or higher — see
  `get_available_transitions` and pick per beat, or use straight cuts.
- Text-only frames because the visual was hard, not because type is the point.
- Motion that makes narration harder to follow.
- A mood or palette that has no connection to the subject.
