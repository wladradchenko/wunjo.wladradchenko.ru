# Storytelling

## The first seconds are a promise

Open with the thing the viewer came for, not with preamble. No logo sting, no
"in this video we will", no history of the field. The first line should state
the tension or the payoff: what is broken, what is surprising, what they will
be able to do.

Then keep the promise you made. A video that opens on a question and never
answers it is worse than one that never asked.

## One argument, in order

Write the spine as three to six beats, each one sentence, and check that each
beat changes what the viewer knows. If two beats can swap places without loss,
they are not beats — they are a list, and lists lose attention.

Reliable spines:

- **Problem → why the obvious fix fails → the actual fix → proof.**
- **Claim → evidence → objection → answer.**
- **Before → the turn → after.**

## Cut the throat-clearing

Every section: delete the first sentence and see whether anything is lost.
Usually it was setup for the speaker's benefit, not the viewer's.

Say numbers and names once, concretely. "Three times faster on a 4K timeline"
survives; "significantly improved performance" does not.

## Give the viewer a reason to stay past each transition

At every section boundary the viewer decides again. Land the previous beat,
then open the next one with the consequence: "which is why the second attempt
failed", not "next, let's talk about attempt two".

## End on the takeaway, not on housekeeping

Close with the one sentence from the brief. Subscribe prompts, credits and
links go after that, or nowhere.

## Working method

Write the script as beats first and put them in the chat for the user to see
before generating any voice or asset. Once approved, mark the beats on the
timeline with `add_marker` so the edit, the b-roll plan and the review all
refer to the same structure.
